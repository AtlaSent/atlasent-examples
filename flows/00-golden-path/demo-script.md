# Golden-path demo — speaker notes

Five scenes, ~4 minutes on a live call. Each block below is what to say
while that scene is on screen; the runner handles the decision output
itself. Press Enter before starting so the terminal is clean.

For a rehearsal, drop the pace: `GOLDEN_PATH_PACE_MS=400 npm start`.

## Scene 1 — Normal production deploy (allow)

> "This is a boring, happy-path production deploy. Senior release
> engineer, tagged release, business hours. AtlaSent evaluates the full
> actor + action + target picture, decides this is low risk, and returns
> a permit. Every prod deploy you ship gets this treatment — the ones
> that should go through feel identical to having no gate at all."

## Scene 2 — After-hours deploy, unfamiliar actor (deny)

> "Same service, same environment. Different story: a contractor
> service account, 2am, no MFA, no prior deploys to this service.
> Notice this isn't a static rule — the risk factors on screen are what
> AtlaSent inferred from context. A static allow-list either blocks the
> contractor forever, or lets this through. We do neither."

## Scene 3 — Step-up recovery (allow)

> "Same contractor, same request, five minutes later — this time with
> MFA and a named approver from the release team. AtlaSent re-evaluates
> and now allows, with a short-lived permit. This is the pattern that
> kills the ‘security team blocks everything so engineers work around
> it’ dynamic: we give you a real recovery path instead of a veto."

## Scene 4 — Regulated LIMS write (allow, audit sealed)

> "Same engine, totally different domain. A lab analyst posts a clinical
> lab result. AtlaSent binds the permit to this exact sample, assay, and
> analyst; the write only commits if that permit is consumed. That's
> your 21 CFR Part 11 audit trail, generated by the same API that just
> gated the deploy — one control plane, every regulated write."

## Scene 5 — Bulk export without override (deny)

> "Same analyst, but now they're exporting ten thousand subject records
> to personal cloud storage. Separation of duties fires — the person
> who writes the data can't also bulk-export it without a second
> approver. AtlaSent denies and tells the analyst exactly what override
> path would unblock them, so this becomes a conversation, not a
> ticket."

## Close

> "Five scenes, one API, one audit trail. Every one of those decisions
> is queryable, replayable, and exportable as evidence. The next step
> is pointing this at one of your real services in staging — takes
> about an hour end to end."
